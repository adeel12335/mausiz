Maurizio Dècina (born 1943) is an Italian electronic engineer and telecommunications scholar whose work has focused on digital communications networks and broadband infrastructure.12 He is professor emeritus of telecommunications at the Politecnico di Milano.1 His career has involved research on digital and broadband networks, participation in international standardisation within the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) and roles in Italian telecommunications policy and infrastructure projects.34

Early life and education

Dècina was born in Italy in 1943.5 He studied electronic engineering at the Sapienza University of Rome under the laurea vecchio ordinamento framework, a five-year programme combining general scientific subjects with specialist courses in electronics, information and communication technologies.6 He graduated in 1966 with a degree in electronic engineering.6

In 1969 he joined SIP (later Telecom Italia and TIM) as a technical officer and executive involved in digitisation of the Italian national telephone network.78

Academic career

Dècina joined the Faculty of Engineering at Sapienza University of Rome in 1976 and in 1980 was appointed full professor of telecommunications.9 In 1986 he moved to the Politecnico di Milano, taking up a chair in telecommunications in the Department of Electronics and Information (later the Department of Electronics, Information and Bioengineering, DEIB).12 At Politecnico di Milano he taught courses on switching architectures, information theory, cryptography and network protocols, and supervised undergraduate and doctoral students in telecommunications engineering.310

He retired from regular teaching duties in 2012.2 In 2014, on the recommendation of the academic senate of the Politecnico di Milano, the Ministry of Education, Universities and Research conferred on him the title of professor emeritus of telecommunications.1

Visiting positions and laboratory consulting

During his academic career, Dècina held visiting and consulting positions at several industrial research laboratories. In 1981–1982 and 1990 he was a resident expert consultant at AT&T Bell Laboratories in Naperville and Chicago, where he worked on experimental voice and data packet-switching systems.11 He also worked with the IBM Research Laboratory in Zurich on routing architectures and local area network protocols.8

From 1983 to 1986 he was director of the Central Research Laboratories at Italtel, overseeing research and development activities and the industrial implementation of digital telephone exchanges based on the Linea UT system.12

Research and international standardisation

Dècina's research has covered digital transmission over copper, coaxial cable and early fibre-optic networks, as well as switching architectures for broadband communications.1310 He has published on packet-switched voice and data communications and on asynchronous transfer mode (ATM) as a technology for broadband integrated services digital networks.13

From 1972 to 1988 he was a member of the Italian delegation to the International Telecommunication Union in Geneva and served as vice-chair of CCITT (now ITU-T) Study Group XVIII, which worked on standards for digital networks, voice coding, signalling systems and Integrated Services Digital Network (ISDN).4 From 1988 to 1994 he directed Subproject 4 of the National Research Council (CNR) targeted project "Telecommunications", which focused on voice and data ATM switching system test beds and linked Italian academic research to European Union research programmes.14

Entrepreneurial and advisory ventures

In addition to his university posts, Dècina has worked as a consultant and entrepreneur. In 1998 he founded ICT Consulting in Milan, a company specialising in telecommunications engineering, network planning and regulatory consulting for corporate and public clients.8 In 2001 he founded Securmatics, a firm providing managed security services and network monitoring; the company was later incorporated by Italtel.15

He has served as an independent member of the boards of Telecom Italia (TIM), Italtel and SIA (Nexi) and has been a member of advisory boards for organisations including Boston Technologies, Kiwi Venture Partners and Fondazione Accenture Italia.816

Regulatory leadership and public policy

Dècina has advised Italian public authorities on telecommunications and information technology policy. From 1989 to 1998 he was a senior consultant to the Authority for IT in Public Administration (AIPA), contributing to projects on the computerisation and networking of ministerial offices.817

Between 1999 and 2003 he worked as an external regulatory consultant to the Italian Communications Authority. He returned to AGCOM as a commissioner from 2012 to 2013 and took part in work on wholesale access pricing, local loop unbundling and online intellectual property enforcement.17 From 2002 to 2004 he was senior technical advisor to the Italian Minister for Innovation and Technologies, working on the design and implementation of the Public Connectivity System (SPC) for government networks.8

From 2007 to 2008 he was president of the Ugo Bordoni Foundation, which acts as a scientific advisor to the Ministry of Economic Development.183

Broadband deployment and NRRP

From 2016 to 2020 Dècina was president of Infratel Italia, an in-house company of the Ministry of Economic Development responsible for implementing the National Ultra-Broadband Plan (Piano BUL) in underserved "white areas".1920

From 2021 to 2022 he was an expert consultant to the Minister for Technological Innovation and Digital Transition in the context of the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP), working on tenders for the "Italia a 1 Giga" and "Italia 5G" connectivity initiatives.21

Educational legacy and CEFRIEL

In 1986 Dècina founded CEFRIEL (Center of Research and Training in Information Engineering) in Milan, an academic–industrial partnership supported by information technology companies, universities and regional authorities, and served as its scientific director until 2003.22 CEFRIEL provides postgraduate education and carries out technology transfer and joint research projects in information engineering.22

Politecnico di Milano has included his career in its digital heritage initiatives; interviews and technical records are preserved in the DEIB Historical Collections and the "Polimuseo" virtual museum.3
